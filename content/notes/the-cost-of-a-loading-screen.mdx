Every portfolio has a preloader now. A wordmark, a progress bar counting to 100,
a word like `INITIALIZING` in letter-spaced caps. I like them. I built one for
this site. But it is worth being honest about what they cost.

## The progress bar is usually lying

Most preloader progress bars are not measuring anything. They animate from 0 to
100 on a timer, then wait for a fixed duration before dismissing. The number is
decoration. That is fine, right up until the timer is longer than the page took
to load — at which point you have added latency to your own site and called it
polish.

I tested a portfolio recently where the preloader sat at "100% — INITIALIZING"
for six seconds on a cold load. The page underneath was ready. The visitor was
being held at the door by an animation that had already finished.

## What a preloader is actually for

There is a real job here: hiding a flash of unstyled text while a display font
loads, and covering the moment where a hero image pops in. Both are genuine, and
both are short. If your preloader is doing that job, it should be measured in
hundreds of milliseconds.

So the rules I settled on:

- **Cap it.** A hard ceiling, dismissed early if the page is ready sooner.
- **Once per session.** A returning visitor has your fonts cached. Showing them
  the boot sequence again is theatre at their expense.
- **Never gate the content.** Render the page and put the preloader over it. If
  the JavaScript fails, the visitor still gets the site rather than a permanently
  frozen splash screen.
- **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Not "make it faster" — skip it entirely.

## The one that matters most

That third rule is the one people skip. If your preloader is a wrapper that
returns `null` until a state flag flips, then every crawler that does not execute
JavaScript sees nothing, and every visitor whose bundle fails to load sees
nothing. An overlay costs you the same effort and fails open instead of closed.

A loading screen is a small pleasure. It should not be a toll gate.
